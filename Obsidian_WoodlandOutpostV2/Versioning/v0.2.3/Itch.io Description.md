# Woodland Outpost

**Explore the forest. Manage your supplies. Build a shelter before winter arrives.**

Woodland Outpost is a compact 2D survival-management game made with Godot.

You arrive in the middle of a procedurally generated wilderness with only a small camp and a limited amount of time. Search the woodland for berries, timber, and stone, carry your supplies home, manage your hunger, and gradually transform the camp into a cabin capable of surviving the winter.

## Plan every expedition

Resources are spread across a large forest, and not everything can be found close to camp.

Wood and food are available near the starting area, while stone generally requires longer expeditions. An off-screen indicator will help you find the way home—but deciding when to return is up to you.

Your backpack can carry a maximum of **12 kg**, and every resource has a different weight:

- Food: 1 kg
- Wood: 2 kg
- Stone: 3 kg

Press **Tab** to inspect the backpack. Every kilogram is represented by a physical space, and larger resources visibly occupy multiple connected cells.

Do you carry emergency food, leave more room for building materials, continue exploring, or return to camp with what you already have?

## Keep hunger under control

Hunger gradually decreases as time passes. If it reaches zero, your woodland adventure comes to a rather embarrassing end.

Food is not consumed automatically: you must decide when to eat it. Carrying berries makes an expedition safer, but every unit of food also occupies backpack space that could have been used for wood or stone.

The Hunger display and its warnings help you judge whether you can gather one more resource—or whether it is finally time to stop being brave and eat something.

## Gathering takes time

Gathering is not instantaneous.

Hold the interaction key while your character works and watch the progress above the resource. Different materials require different amounts of time, and interrupted work is preserved.

You can leave an unfinished resource, return later, and continue from where you stopped.

Gathered resources also leave traces in the world: trees become stumps, empty bushes remain behind, and exhausted rocks leave holes in the ground.

## Manage your camp

Returning to camp now opens a dedicated management screen.

Your backpack appears on the left, while camp storage and construction information appear on the right. You can deposit individual resources, unload everything at once, or retrieve stored supplies to prepare your next expedition.

Construction uses resources stored at camp, so building the cabin is no longer an automatic transaction. You can clearly see what you own, what the next stage requires, and what you may still need to find.

## Build before winter

The days continue to pass while you explore, gather, and travel.

Develop the camp through multiple construction stages and complete the final cabin before winter arrives. If time expires—or Hunger reaches zero—the expedition is lost.

A successful run requires more than collecting everything you see. You must decide what to carry, when to eat, when to return, and which resources matter most right now.

## Features

- A large procedurally generated woodland.
- Dense decorative vegetation and environmental details.
- Distance-based resource distribution.
- Guaranteed resource quantities that keep generated worlds winnable.
- Timed and resumable gathering.
- Resource-specific gathering sounds, animations, and visual effects.
- A visible Hunger system with manual eating.
- A visual 12 kg backpack with weighted resource shapes.
- A dedicated camp-management interface.
- Manual deposits and resource retrieval.
- Progressive camp construction.
- A complete day-and-night cycle.
- Navigation assistance for finding the camp.
- Contextual instructions for new players.
- Persistent depleted-resource visuals.
- Multiple approaches to planning each expedition.

## Controls

Move - WASD or arrow keys

Gather / interact - Hold E

Open / close backpack - Tab

Eat carried Food - F

Close interface / pause - Esc

Toggle fullscreen - Alt + Enter

## Current version

**Version 0.2.3 — Hunger & Camp Management**

This update makes survival and resource planning much more visible. Hunger is now an active concern, the backpack shows exactly how resources consume its limited capacity, and the new camp screen gives the player direct control over stored and carried supplies.

## Development status

I know that Woodland Outpost is still a small and slightly silly game—but that is part of the fun!

This project is an experiment and, most importantly, my way of learning how to make games with Godot. I’m building it one mechanic at a time, testing ideas, making mistakes, occasionally creating a much bigger system because I decided to display one little number, and gradually turning everything into something more complete and enjoyable.

The artwork is still simple and currently relies partly on AI-assisted assets because visual art is definitely not my strongest skill. I would love to improve its consistency and personality as the project continues to grow.

There is still plenty I want to experiment with, so follow me on this little development journey!

If you play the game and have an idea, encounter a bug, or think something could be more fun, please let me know in the comments. Player feedback has already influenced several important changes, and your suggestions could help shape the next version of Woodland Outpost.
