Ok, my original idea for an update was a little different, and is about the hunger/need to eat part. Basically now the player just need to have some food in the storage, and it's consumed at night. I wish to have someting like an "hunger level", that start from 100 (for example) and decrease during the day, and when this reach 0 the game is lost. The player can eat during the day for restore the hunger level. 

This is a strong idea—and it creates a more natural decision system than the current automatic nightly consumption.

Right now, food is effectively a construction tax:

> Keep at least one food in storage before night.

A hunger meter would turn food into something the player must actively manage while exploring:

> Can I continue gathering stone, or should I eat now?  
> Should I carry food, or use that backpack space for materials?  
> Do I eat this berry now, or bring it back for later?

That fits Woodland Outpost very well.

## Recommended basic system

Call the value `hunger` in the interface, while treating `100` as completely fed:

```
Hunger: 100 / 100
```

It continuously decreases while the game is running:

|Hunger|State|Initial effect|
|---|---|---|
|61–100|Well fed|Normal|
|31–60|Hungry|Yellow HUD warning|
|1–30|Starving|Red HUD warning and heartbeat|
|0|Collapsed|Game over|

For the first implementation, I would avoid movement or gathering penalties. Losing at zero already provides pressure, while slowing a starving player can create an unpleasant death spiral.

We can add a mild penalty later if testing shows hunger is too easy to ignore.

## Suggested starting balance

Your current day lasts approximately 90 seconds, with seven days before winter. To remain close to the existing requirement of about seven food:

```
maximum_hunger := 100.0
hunger_decrease_per_second := 1.0
food_hunger_restoration := 80.0
```

This means:

- A full hunger meter lasts approximately 100 seconds.
- One food restores 80 hunger.
- The player normally needs approximately one food per day.
- Starting at 100 provides a small opening-day buffer.
- Approximately seven food units are still needed across a complete game.

These are excellent prototype numbers because they preserve your current food balance surprisingly closely.

## Eating should use carried food

The most interesting version is:

- Away from camp, the player can eat only food in the backpack.
- At camp, the player can eat from the backpack or camp storage.
- One food still weighs 1 kg.
- Eating restores hunger up to 100.
- The existing automatic nightly food consumption is removed.

That creates a genuine expedition decision. Carrying two food means less room for wood and stone, but it allows the player to remain away from camp longer.

For example:

```
Backpack 12 / 12 kg

2 Food  = 2 kg
2 Wood  = 4 kg
2 Stone = 6 kg
```

The player is prepared for a long expedition, but the entire backpack is already committed.

## Prevent accidental food waste

If food restores 80 points, eating at 90 hunger would waste most of it. That could technically be a choice, but it will usually feel like an input mistake.

I recommend disabling eating above perhaps `85` hunger:

```
You are not hungry enough to eat.
```

Below that value, the prompt could explain the result:

```
F — Eat Food (+80 Hunger)
```

When no food is carried:

```
No Food in Backpack
```

At camp:

```
F — Eat from Storage
```

## HUD presentation

A horizontal bar beneath the top HUD would work well:

```
HUNGER  ███████████████░░░░░  74
```

Its colour can change gradually:

- Green above 60
- Gold between 30 and 60
- Red below 30

The interface does not need to display decimals. Internally hunger can be a `float`, while the HUD displays a rounded integer.

When it becomes low, show one clear milestone warning:

```
YOU ARE GETTING HUNGRY
Find food or return to camp
```

Avoid showing it continuously. Trigger it only when crossing a threshold.

## Why this creates meaningful decisions

This system connects several existing mechanics:

- Time determines hunger loss.
- Exploration determines how far the player is from stored food.
- Backpack weight determines how much emergency food can be carried.
- Resource distribution determines whether gathering berries is worth a detour.
- Camp storage provides safety.
- Construction competes indirectly with survival time.

Most importantly, food is no longer something the player deposits and forgets. It becomes an active expedition resource.

## One important warning

We should remove the nightly food consumption when hunger is introduced. Keeping both systems would charge the player twice:

- Hunger decreases continuously.
- Storage also loses food at night.

That would be confusing and would substantially disrupt the balance you currently like.

Weather could later modify hunger rather than adding another consumption system:

```
Cold day: hunger decreases 25% faster.
Rain: hunger decreases 10% faster.
Campfire: hunger decreases more slowly near camp.
```

This would connect beautifully with the forecast system we discussed, but only after the basic hunger loop is enjoyable.

My recommendation is to make this the next prototype. It creates an immediate, understandable decision without adding an artificial menu at the beginning of every day.
